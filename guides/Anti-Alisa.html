Movement — Which Way to Step

Default: Sidestep RIGHT (SSR). The TekkenDocs anti-strat Quick overview (community-written) says: "Small poking and movement, turns into a pressure monster in chainsaw stance (DES)… SSR. SWL against chainsaws (d1, f2,1, ... requires SWL). DES b2 tracks." The @Mezerdie sidestep chart (via sidestep.md) also says SSR, so the two community sources agree.

Exceptions — the chainsaws flip it:
• Against her chainsaw (Weapon) moves the page says to sidewalk LEFT instead. It names d1 and f2,1. Alisa has no plain d+1, so d1 is read here as DES.d+1 (Hertz Blade - Scud). f2,1 could be the standing f+2,1 or DES.f+2,1; Wavu tags both as Weapon moves.
• DES.b+2 "tracks" — don't step it, block it (-12, punish).
• Nine moves are homing (list below), including her two Heat Engagers 3,2 and uf+2 and her fast high 4. Stepping does nothing against them — but 3,2's 2nd hit, uf+2 and 4 are all highs, so duck them.

So the habit is: SSR in neutral and against her standing pokes; once she's holding the chainsaws (DES), switch to SWL or just block. Wavu's per-move notes carry only one step note (2,3,4, either side), so there is no Tier 1 data to confirm or contradict the direction.

Stance Check — can a jab interrupt her plus stance entries?

Arithmetic only, all from Wavu. Effective startup = her raw startup − her frame advantage. Your jab is 10f, so effective i9 or faster → jab can't interrupt; exactly i10 → trades; i11 or slower → a 10f jab interrupts. Where Wavu gives a range (i13~18), the fastest frame is used. Only entries with a stated plus frame advantage on block are covered; minus entries (3,2 -2 into DES, f+2,1 -12, …) leave you the turn anyway.
Mike's rule: a character can't block in a unique stance unless a source says otherwise. Here sources do: the anti-strat page says she can block from DES by putting the chainsaws away (1f), and Wavu says DBT can cancel to standing block with B. Nothing says SBT can block.
Hit-level and state notes change the read: a standing jab is a high, so it whiffs against a crouching move (DES.d+1 crouches from frame 4) and Wavu gives DES.2 jab evasion. No DES, SBT or DBT attack is listed as a power crush.

Lab drill — training wheels: Two drills, because her step answer changes with stance. (1) Stage 1 — dummy throws a standalone 1 (jab); SSR right after blocking it, 20-rep sets, until 18/20 on three straight sets (direction only, timing untested). (2) Stage 1 — dummy throws a standalone f+2; SWL right after blocking it, same graduation. Stage 2 — the real f+2,1 and DES.d+1 with randomized timing, one branch at a time. Stage 3 — mix f+2,1 with f+2,3 (high ender — duck instead) and plain f+2 (-4, nothing to step), plus DES.b+2, which the anti-strat page says tracks, so the step has to become a read. Weaknesses: a standalone opener has no follow-up on a clock, recovers differently from the same hit inside a string, and knowing it's coming removes the read. Rep counts are a heuristic. Cap a session at around 100–150 reps, stop if accuracy falls 15 points, and go back a stage if a stage drops under 60% for two sets.
