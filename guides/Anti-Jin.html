Jin on P1 side — inputs face right

Movement — Which Way to Step

Default: Sidestep / sidewalk RIGHT (SSR / SWR). This is the strongest-evidenced step default in the database so far — it agrees across all three sources. TekkenDocs' community sidestep chart (by @Mezerdie) calls SSR for Jin, and Wavu's own per-move notes back it seven-to-two: seven of his strings are documented as whiffing to your right, two to your left. It's also what the SSR/SWR Default tag in the title above refers to.

The cost: his mid b,f+2 is specifically noted as having good left sidestep tracking. It punishes the wrong habit, not this one, so the default is cheap to hold.

The payoff case: see the df+2~DF crouch-dash mixup under General Counter Play. Three of its four branches are steppable right and the fourth is slow, which is what makes SSR → delayed block work as a single option select covering the whole mixup.

Standing Block Punishment

Two moves look alike and aren't: plain df+2 (Wind Hook Fist, CD.df+2) is a naked HIGH at i11~12, -10 on block — but too fast to duck on reaction, so block it standing and punish. The electric version (df#2 / EWHF) is a completely different animal: +5~+6 on block. Don't assume every Wind Hook Fist is free.

Entered via (backward step, actionable from frame 5) or (forward step, actionable from frame 10).

Can he block from a held Zenshin stance? No source confirms this either way — not asserting it.

Lows from the stance: just one — at -14 oB. Everything else is mid or high, which is why standing block is the correct default response to Zenshin.

General Counter Play

i3~9 active | Standalone parry
Catches highs AND mids — not a punches-only parry. Beaten clean by throws, unblockable moves, and charge moves. Vulnerable on frames 1-2 and 10-30 of its animation (TekkenDocs); he can block starting frame 31. A well-timed low or a delayed hit during that vulnerable window punishes the attempt hard
i75~76 | Unblockable | Balcony Break
Avenger — genuinely unblockable, but the startup is enormous. Sidestep or interrupt anywhere during the long wind-up rather than trying to block it
Wavu's own counterplay writeup singles out and as Jin's two panic buttons against rushdown pressure — both are already tiered under Standing Block Punishment above (-10~-9 and -16 respectively), but worth calling out together: if you're leaning on him and he throws one of these out, that's the tell his offense is running out of ideas.
Remember the plain vs. electric (EWHF) distinction from the top of Standing Block Punishment: the plain version is -10 and too fast to duck reactively, the electric version is +5~+6 and unpunishable outright. Learn to tell them apart by sound/visual tell, not by trying to react to the number.

df+2~DF mixup — Crouch Dash cancel

Canceling directly into Crouch Dash (df+2~DF, Breaking Step) hands Jin easier access to the electric timings on his Crouch Dash follow-ups than starting Crouch Dash cold. Four button options come out of it:

(Electric / EWHF) +5~6 | High | Tornado launcher

i20~21 | -31 | Low | Hellsweep, knockdown
Steppable right
i16~18 | -9 | Mid | Homing | Bamboo Splitter — slow
NOT steppable — homing. The one option in this mixup that a sidestep alone doesn't solve
Labbed answer: side-step right, then delay your block (SSR~delayed block). The sidestep clears the 1, 2, and 4 options outright since all three are steppable right — and delaying the block afterward still leaves enough time to block the homing 3, since Bamboo Splitter is comparatively slow. In effect, SSR~delayed block is a single option select that answers all four branches of the df+2~DF mixup without needing to read which one is coming.
