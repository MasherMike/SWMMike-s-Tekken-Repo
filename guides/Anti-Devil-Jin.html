Devil Jin on P1 side — inputs face right

Movement — Which Way to Step

Default: Sidestep RIGHT (SSR). Wavu's per-move notes list seven strings that whiff to your right only (3,1,2, 3,1,4, f+4,3, b,f+2,1,2, b,f+2,1,df+2, ws1,1, ws1,4) against one that whiffs left only (1,2,3,4, from the 3rd block). The TekkenDocs anti-strat page agrees: "After jab, SSR block. df1 can be SSR'd consistently."

Exceptions — these catch the SSR habit:
• df+2 (i15, -9) "has good right sidestep tracking" — and on hit he can hold F into MCR at +14.
• b+1 (i12 high, -6) "tracks SSR/SWR completely". It's high, so duck it instead.
• The hellsweep (CD.DF+4): the anti-strat page says you "have to SSL CD4". It's a low, so blocking it low is the cleaner answer (-23c).
• uf+3+4 is "pseudo homing to SSL/SWL side" — it catches a step left, so it doesn't punish your default.
• Six moves are homing (list below) — stepping does nothing.

Tier disagreement: the @Mezerdie sidestep chart (via sidestep.md) says SSL for Devil Jin. The per-move notes (Tier 1) and the anti-strat page both point right, so this guide follows them.

Situational steps — TekkenDocs anti-strat (community-written), verify in lab

These come from the anti-strat page's Quick overview, not from per-move notes. The page writes frame numbers from your side; the Wavu frames are given alongside.
• After a jab: SSR, then block.
• After uf+3 on block (Wavu: he's +3c): SSL beats df+1 and df+2; SSR beats df+1, df+4, df+2 and 1,2. The page lists df+2 on both sides, and Wavu says df+2 tracks right — treat df+2 as unsteppable here.
• After db+2 on hit (Wavu: he's +3c): SSR beats ws4 and ws2; SSL beats ws1; SWL beats uf4.
• After WR3 / f,f,F+3 on block (Wavu: +6~+9): "hard to step"; SWL beats CD4.
• Crouch dash: you can SSR CD2, but it's harder; SSL CD4.
• From an on-block MCR entry you can sidestep MCR.1 either way.

Stance Check — can a jab interrupt MCR?

Arithmetic only, all from Wavu. Effective startup = his raw startup − his frame advantage. Your jab is 10f, so effective i9 or faster → jab can't interrupt; exactly i10 → trades; i11 or slower → a 10f jab interrupts. Where Wavu gives a range (i15~16), the fastest frame is used.
Mike's rule: a character can't block in a unique stance unless a source says otherwise. Neither Wavu nor TekkenDocs says Devil Jin can block in MCR, so assume he can't.
Every MCR attack is airborne from frame 1 (Wavu js1~). Theory (Ledger): that crushes lows, so the interrupt has to be a high or a mid. None of the MCR moves is listed as a power crush or as crushing highs. Wavu also notes MCR moves "can be delayed by 10f" — a delay only makes the jab interrupt easier.

Lab (Mike): nothing in this guide is lab-tested yet. Mike's queue note (24 Oct 2026): he lost two 0-3 sets to a player who "opened up stance mixups" — the Stance Counterplay and Stance Check sections are the place to start.
Lab drill — training wheels: Stage 1 — set the dummy to a standalone 1 (jab) and SSR right after blocking it, 20-rep sets, until 18/20 on three straight sets (direction only, timing untested). Then a standalone df+1 with SSR, which the anti-strat page says works "consistently". Stage 2 — the real 3,1,2 and b,f+2,1,2 with randomized timing, stepping right after the first block (both whiff vs SSR). Stage 3 — mix in df+2 and b+1, which track SSR, so the step has to become a read. Weaknesses: a standalone opener has no follow-up on a clock, recovers differently from the same hit inside a string, and knowing it's coming removes the read. Rep counts are a heuristic. Cap a session at around 100–150 reps, stop if accuracy falls 15 points, and go back a stage if a stage drops under 60% for two sets.
