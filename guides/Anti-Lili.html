Lili on P1 side — inputs face right

Movement — Which Way to Step

Default: Sidestep / sidewalk RIGHT (SSR / SWR) — and this one disagrees with the chart. The @Mezerdie sidestep chart calls SSL for Lili. Wavu's per-move data points the other way, six to nothing: six of her strings are documented whiffing to your right, and not one is documented whiffing specifically to your left. Seven more whiff to either side. Under this database's evidence rule, the per-move notes (Tier 1) outrank the chart (Tier 2), so this doc goes right. It's the only doc so far where the two sources directly contradict — worth a lab session to settle.

Step a lot. Wavu's counterplay page opens with it: "without frequent sidestepping, fighting Lili can be an absolute pain," because so many of her moves are linear. The plan is to step until she reaches for her slower tracking options, then punish those.

The hard exception: never step her backturn. Out of BT, both her mid (BT.3) and her fast low (BT.d+2) track and high-crush — Wavu: "avoid doing highs or stepping!" Backdash and crouch-block instead (see Stance Counterplay). And duck, don't step, DEW.3+4, which Wavu calls her best tracking move.

Stance Counterplay

BT — Backturn (Mirror Image)

Entered raw with b+3+4 (actionable after 16F), and off 1,1 (-6), df+3+4 backflip (-4~+1), 3 (-11), f+1+2 with B (-3), b+2,1 with B (-4), df+4,4 with B (-9), uf+4 with B, and on hit off d+1,2 and f,F+4 (+10). Unlike the other stances in this database, BT has a lot of lows — that's what makes it a real mixup. Can she block in BT? Wavu doesn't say directly; its advice is to check her with a mid or low when she isn't plus.

How to deal with it — the fuzzy guard: Wavu's answer is to backdash, then crouch-block. Her two fast options, BT.3 and BT.d+2, are short-range, so the backdash makes them whiff. The move that catches backdashing is the slower, longer BT.d+3+4 sweep — so crouch-block in time for it. "At most ranges, backdashing into crouch guard will beat all backturn mix." If she isn't plus, Wavu says a ~i16 mid interrupts the 1,1 and backflip entries; after d+1,2 on hit, crouch jab beats both follow-ups. High-level Lilis also sit in BT to bait a press, since her crouching exit from BT slides her far enough back to make most moves whiff.
